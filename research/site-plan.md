# The Window Brief — Master Site Plan

Status: working master plan  
Primary audience: Australian homeowners first, then architects/builders  
Primary language: Australian English  
Core positioning: Australian homes first. European technology where it makes sense.

## Editorial guardrail

The Window Brief must never feel like Europe teaching Australia how to build.

The reader should feel:
1. this is about my Australian home;
2. my problem is understood first;
3. European technology is one possible tool;
4. local Australian conditions and requirements decide whether it fits;
5. the house remains Australian in character, lifestyle and identity.

Core line:
> The technology can be European. The home stays Australian.

Supporting line:
> Still your Australian home. Just better to live in.

---

# User journey — the staircase

## 1. FEEL
What do I want to improve in my home?

Entry points:
- Cooler in summer
- Warmer in winter
- Quieter inside
- Less draughty
- Safer and more secure
- Larger openings
- Keep the character of my home
- More comfortable living

## 2. UNDERSTAND
Why does this happen?

Explain the problem without jargon:
- heat gain and heat loss
- air leakage and sealing
- sound paths
- glazing basics
- opening types
- frame and sash behaviour
- installation interface

## 3. DISCOVER
What other window and door solutions exist?

Introduce European technology only here:
- tilt & turn
- casement
- fixed glazing
- lift & slide
- entrance doors
- thermally broken aluminium
- uPVC systems
- glazing options
- hardware and multipoint locking

## 4. VERIFY
Does it make sense for my Australian home?

Australian context:
- climate zone
- orientation and solar exposure
- coastal conditions
- bushfire / BAL
- wind exposure
- flyscreens and security screens
- architectural character
- COLORBOND-style colour context
- local installation details
- AS 2047 / AS 1288 / NCC where relevant
- builder / architect compatibility

## 5. CHOOSE
What should I actually specify?

Decision support:
- compare frame materials
- compare opening styles
- double vs triple glazing
- glazing by goal
- thresholds
- screens
- colours and finishes
- practical specification checklists

## 6. GET ADVICE
Talk about the actual project.

Project Brief:
- postcode / location
- new build / renovation / replacement
- house style
- plans / photos / schedule if available
- priorities
- constraints
- route to appropriate product / supplier / partner

# Editorial rules that protect the Australian positioning

Do:
- say "for Australian homes"
- lead with homeowner outcomes
- respect Australian architecture
- show Australian climates and lifestyles
- explain when European technology does NOT make sense
- use Australian terminology
- discuss flyscreens, BAL, coastal, COLORBOND context and installation
- use real technical evidence
- separate European product data from Australian compliance evidence

Avoid:
- "European windows are better"
- "Australian windows are inferior"
- suggesting that European standards replace Australian requirements
- using European house imagery as the dominant visual language
- leading with RAL/EN/DIN jargon
- assuming triple glazing is always best
- treating Australia as one climate
- making imported origin itself the selling point
