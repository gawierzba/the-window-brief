# The Window Brief — Master Site Plan

Status: working master plan — WA-first revision  
Launch market: Western Australia, with Perth / Fremantle as the operational centre  
Future architecture: Australia-ready  
Primary audience: WA homeowners first, then architects and builders  
Primary language: Australian English

## Strategic position

The Window Brief is not a European window catalogue translated for Australia.

It is a Western Australian-facing expert guide that starts with the home, the site and the owner's problem, then introduces European window and door technology only where it is useful.

Primary promise:
> Better windows for Western Australian homes.

Core philosophy:
> Australian homes first. European technology where it makes sense.

Emotional reassurance:
> The technology can be European. The home stays Australian.

Supporting line:
> Still your Australian home. Just better to live in.

Operating model:
> WA-first, Australia-ready.

---

# The reader journey — six steps

The whole site should behave like a staircase. The reader is never forced into technical depth.

## 1. FEEL — What do I want my home to feel like?

Start with lived experience, not products.

Primary entry points:
- Cooler in summer
- More comfortable in winter
- Quieter inside
- Less draughty
- Safer and more secure
- Larger openings
- Better control of sun and weather
- Keep the character of my home

## 2. UNDERSTAND — Why is this happening?

Explain the cause in plain Australian English:
- solar heat gain
- heat loss
- air leakage
- weak seals
- sound paths
- glazing behaviour
- frame behaviour
- opening type
- installation interface

## 3. DISCOVER — What other ways are there to build a window?

Only now introduce European technology:
- tilt & turn
- casement
- fixed glazing
- lift & slide
- entrance doors
- thermally broken aluminium
- uPVC
- insulating glass units
- acoustic glazing
- multipoint locking
- low thresholds
- hardware and sealing systems

European engineering is a toolbox, not a cultural argument.

## 4. VERIFY — Does it suit this Western Australian property?

Bring every solution back to the site:
- Perth / WA climate
- solar exposure and orientation
- coastal salt and corrosion
- bushfire-prone areas and BAL where applicable
- aircraft and traffic noise where applicable
- wind and weather exposure
- flyscreens and security screens
- architectural character
- colours and finishes familiar in WA
- local installation details
- AS 2047 / AS 1288 / NCC where relevant
- builder and architect interface

Never imply that every WA property has every exposure.

## 5. CHOOSE — What direction makes sense?

Help the reader make sense of:
- frame material
- opening style
- glass configuration
- double vs triple glazing
- acoustic options
- hardware
- thresholds
- screens
- colours and finishes
- project-specific trade-offs

## 6. ASK — Apply it to my actual project

Soft expert conversion:
- postcode / suburb
- new build / renovation / replacement
- house style
- plans / photos / window schedule
- priorities
- site exposures
- current project stage

Primary CTA:
> Tell me about your project.

Secondary CTA:
> Not sure what makes sense? Ask.

The commercial pathway appears only after the expert conversation:
technical review → recommended direction → suitable supply / distribution / local support pathway.

# Mandatory Australian / WA editorial rules

Do:
- begin with the Australian home
- use Australian English
- write for WA conditions at launch
- respect Federation and other Australian architectural character
- explain project-specific trade-offs
- say when European technology may not be necessary
- discuss screens, sun, coast, bushfire and local installation context naturally
- distinguish product performance evidence from Australian compliance
- use real drawings and real systems as evidence
- keep commercial pathways transparent but secondary

Avoid:
- "European windows are better"
- "Australian windows are inferior"
- cultural superiority
- fake Australian slang
- dominant imagery of European houses
- leading with DIN / EN / RAL jargon
- presenting triple glazing as universally superior
- treating all of WA as one identical exposure
- treating all of Australia as one climate
- implying a European certificate replaces Australian requirements
- turning the homepage into an importer catalogue

---

# Final publishing test

Before any page goes live, ask:

1. Does this sound like it was written for a Western Australian home?
2. Does the opening begin with the reader's life rather than our product?
3. Does it respect Australian architecture and lifestyle?
4. Is European technology introduced as a useful option rather than a superior culture?
5. Are WA site conditions used accurately and only where relevant?
6. Are technical claims supportable?
7. Is Australian compliance separated from European product data?
8. Does the page teach something useful before asking for contact?
9. Is the CTA expert-first rather than sales-first?
10. Would the page still be valuable if the reader never bought from us?
11. Would the homeowner still feel: "This is my Australian home"?

If any answer is no, revise before publishing.
